# Limitations of the State of the Community Survey 2026


## 1. The sample is not representative of WordPress users

It should not be described as such by us or by anyone citing it.

This sample is heavily weighted toward long-tenured, professionally invested participants: **55.0% have been in the community for a decade or more, and only six respondents have been around less than two years.** Findings about the community *core* are well supported. Findings about WordPress's broader user base are not available from this instrument, and we make none.

## 2. Self-selection during a period of public conflict

A community survey fielded while that community is being affected by public conflict will attract engaged respondents on all sides. We cannot rule this out.

However, response patterns vary sensibly across items and do not show uniform negativity: the same respondents reporting the most negative WordPress sentiment also report +78 net positivity toward open source and 95.1% agreement that Security in WordPress matters.

## 3. The "AI in WordPress" importance item is bimodal

**Do not quote this item on its own.**

Of the 55 respondents who rated "AI in WordPress" *very important*, **13 are negative or very negative about AI in WordPress** — 11 of them *very* negative. They rated it important because they consider it an important **problem**.

Two respondents identified this flaw independently and told us so in free text before we had analyzed anything. The more accurate measure of attitude toward AI in WordPress is the sentiment item, _net −33_, not the importance item.

**This item is being rewritten for 2027.**

## 4. Questions that returned no data

| Item | Status |
|---|---|
| "Have you personally experienced any of the following when contributing to the WordPress community?" and all seven of its options plus its free-text follow-up (9 columns) | **Entirely empty.** Routing or export fault. The dedicated contribution-friction question is unanalysable for 2026. |
| "What made you uncomfortable asking for help?" and "What else made you uncomfortable asking for help?" | **Entirely empty.** These would have explained the survey's clearest psychological-safety finding. |
| "What issues have you personally encountered when contributing to the WordPress community?" | **Entirely empty.** |

## 5. Export irregularities

- **All published figures are deduplicated.** Two AI use-case columns were duplicated in the export, and one column labelled "Other" was mislabelled. This did not affect the labels the respondents saw.
- **333 respondents answered "yes, my sentiment changed" but 356 supplied a direction of change.** A routing gap. All published figures use the direction item, which has the larger base: 331 negative, 25 positive.

## 6. Question design faults we intend to fix

- **Accessibility, health and safety precautions, and destination-country safety were not offered as closed responses on the event-attendance question** This omission was raised unprompted in their free-written comments multiple respondents. 
- **Some questions were forced-response when they should not have been.** This was primarily a platform limitation.
- The gender question offered "Other" with a free-text follow-up that only one person used. That answer cannot be published without risking respondent identification.

## 7. Group sizes

Non-binary respondents number **15** — exactly at our minimum threshold. Every figure involving this group is excluded from significance testing. It cannot support inference, but we report it because omitting it entirely would be a form of erasure.

Several country-level and role-level cells are similarly small and are not reported as groups.

## 8. What we deliberately did not publish

Row-level response data, and any free-text responses from respondents who did not grant explicit permission.
